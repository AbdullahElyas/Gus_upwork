Ankle Conclusion:

Ankle Assessment Conclusion: Your left foot is in a slightly everted position, with the centre of mass medial to the 2nd metatarsal, indicating a tendency towards pronation. While the foot can access a pronated shape, there is a notable limitation in actual pronation capability, suggesting restricted midfoot articulation or fascial control. Dorsiflexion range is poor, although strength is maintained, which may indicate tightness in the posterior chain or reduced joint mobility. Plantarflexion demonstrates both good range and strength, but further strength development is recommended, particularly through overcoming isometric exercises. There is also a need to enhance proprioceptive awareness and fine motor control of the foot.

The right foot mirrors the left, also presenting in a slightly everted position with similar pronation and supination capabilities. Dorsiflexion is again limited in range but retains good strength, while plantarflexion is strong and unrestricted. No significant differences in range or force production were observed between the two sides.

Foot and Ankle summary: There is a high degree of symmetry between the left and right feet in both position and functional capacity. The key findings are the restricted dorsiflexion range bilaterally and the inability to fully access pronation, despite the feet being positioned in a slightly everted stance. Prioritising overcoming isometric strengthening for plantarflexion, alongside targeted mobility work for dorsiflexion, will be beneficial. Emphasise midfoot articulation and fascial control through dynamic, subconscious movement patterns, such as barefoot gait retraining and controlled balance drills. This approach will help lengthen tissue, improve proprioception, and enhance overall foot function.
