Ankle Conclusion:

Ankle Assessment Conclusion: The Left foot: Your left foot was observed in a slightly everted position, with the centre of mass medial to the 2nd metatarsal. Despite the ability to access both pronation and supination, you were unable to effectively pronate during assessment, indicating a lack of dynamic control through the medial arch. Both dorsiflexion and plantarflexion demonstrated poor range and strength, suggesting significant deficits in both joint mobility and muscular capacity. There was limited articulation through the midfoot and reduced fascial engagement, which may be contributing to the overall lack of control and awareness in this limb.

The Right foot: The right foot was positioned neutrally, with the centre of mass directly over the 2nd metatarsal. Like the left, the right foot could not pronate during testing, but it did display good strength in dorsiflexion despite poor range. Plantarflexion, however, was limited in both range and strength, mirroring the deficits seen on the left but with slightly better dorsiflexion force.

Foot and Ankle summary: There is a clear asymmetry between your left and right feet, particularly in dorsiflexion and plantarflexion range and strength, with the left foot being more compromised overall. Both feet lack effective pronation during movement, and there is a generalised reduction in midfoot articulation and fascial control. The key findings are poor mobility and strength, especially in plantarflexion, and a need for improved proprioceptive awareness. We recommend a structured isometric strengthening programme, focusing on both dorsiflexion and plantarflexion, alongside exercises to enhance subconscious midfoot articulation and fascial lengthening. Incorporating dynamic, weight-bearing activities such as controlled floating heel raises and barefoot balance drills will help restore functional symmetry and control.
