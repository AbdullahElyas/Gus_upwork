The Right foot: The right foot is in a neutral position, with the centre of mass directly over the 2nd metatarsal. Like the left, functional pronation is absent despite the potential for both pronation and supination. Dorsiflexion range and strength are also poor, mirroring the left, but plantarflexion remains strong with good range. The right foot shows slightly better alignment but similar functional deficits.

Foot and Ankle summary: There is a degree of asymmetry, particularly in the left foot’s slightly everted position compared to the right’s neutral alignment, and in dorsiflexion force production. Both feet exhibit poor dorsiflexion range and strength, which will limit effective gait and dynamic stability, despite good plantarflexion capacity. Key findings include restricted midfoot articulation and insufficient fascial engagement, especially during dorsiflexion. We recommend a targeted strengthening programme focusing on overcoming isometrics for dorsiflexion, alongside exercises to enhance midfoot mobility and subconscious movement patterns such as dynamic balance and gait drills. Emphasising tissue lengthening through controlled, subconscious movement will further support improvements in fascial control and overall foot function.
