Ankle Assessment Conclusion: Your left foot presents in a slightly everted position, with the centre of mass medial to the 2nd metatarsal. Plantarflexion, conversely, shows good strength but limited range, highlighting the importance of improving midfoot articulation and fascial mobility to facilitate greater movement.

The right foot mirrors the left in terms of position, also being slightly everted and unable to pronate from its resting state, though it retains the capacity for both pronation and supination. This creates a mild asymmetry, particularly in dorsiflexion force between the two sides.

Foot and Ankle summary: There is a degree of symmetry in foot position and movement potential, but a clear asymmetry exists in dorsiflexion strength, with the left side being weaker. Both feet would benefit from enhanced midfoot articulation and improved fascial control, particularly to facilitate pronation and increase plantarflexion range on the left. Subconscious movement drills, such as dynamic gait retraining and barefoot balance tasks, are recommended to promote tissue lengthening and proprioceptive awareness. Prioritise overcoming isometric strengthening for dorsiflexion and introduce controlled mobility work for plantarflexion, especially on the left. Consistent practice of these strategies will help restore balance and optimise overall foot and ankle function.
