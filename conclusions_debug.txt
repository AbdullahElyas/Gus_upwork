The Right foot: Similarly, your right foot is also in a neutral position and can pronate effectively, but it lacks the ability to supinate. This discrepancy highlights a notable asymmetry between the two feet, particularly in the supination capability and the strength profile of plantarflexion.

Foot and Ankle summary: The asymmetry between your feet is evident, with the left foot exhibiting a balanced capacity for both pronation and supination, while the right foot struggles with supination. The overall poor range and strength in both feet necessitate a focused approach to rehabilitation, particularly through isometric exercises to build foundational strength. Additionally, incorporating exercises that enhance proprioception and control will be beneficial. We recommend integrating dynamic movements that promote awareness and control of the foot and ankle during daily activities, alongside specific strength training for both dorsiflexion and plantarflexion.
